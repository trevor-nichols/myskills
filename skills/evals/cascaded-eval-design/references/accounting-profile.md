# Accounting Extension (Optional Domain Profile)

Use this reference **only** when the task concerns an accounting, tax, audit, reporting, or financial-control evaluation. The general cascade methodology does not assume a workbook, journal entries, or a specific source limit.

## Accounting-specific terminal objects

Recognition/derecognition; appropriate accounting model; scope/entity consolidation; measurement; classification and presentation; selected transaction populations; accrual/settlement state; balances/rollforwards; journal entries; income/OCI/tax effects; regulatory and lender bases; required disclosures; subsequent-event treatment; and final workbook or memo.

## Common accounting forks

- Economic/legal effective date versus operational posting/cash/knowledge date.
- Contract amendment/side-letter priority and whether an obligation is conditional.
- The valid entity, customer, cost, claim, loan, insured population, or transaction scope.
- Different recognition or classification model after resolving a state condition.
- Purpose-specific basis distinctions (book, tax, regulatory, statutory, lender).
- A corrected primary calculation that independently changes a later contractual or regulatory test.
- Net amount that ties while assets/liabilities, payers/payees, or statement components are wrong.

## Required accounting integrity when relevant

Verify appropriate standards and facts; source hierarchy; units, signs and precision; period cutoffs; journal-entry balance; source-to-output ties; population exceptions; opening-to-ending rollforwards; period activity separate from ending balance; disclosure conclusions; and difference between bases.

The original high-difficulty requirements (including >=6 qualified DPs, >=60% dependent and a four-edge chain, several fully calculated ablations, and specialized population/second-order conditions) appear in `difficulty-profiles.md`. Apply stricter industry authority rules when the specific accounting project requires them.

## Domain-specific evidence and golden artifacts

Common solver sources: contracts/amendments, general ledger and subledger, custodian or trustee reports, statutory or tax elections, valuations, event logs, approvals, and operational schedules. Model-generated workpapers do not substitute for controlling underlying source evidence.

A golden accounting artifact should make each upstream conclusion traceable through affected balances, entries, disclosures, and audit checks; it should also document intentionally unaffected accounts/components. Source/workbook conventions remain specific to the user's accounting project rather than part of this general skill.
